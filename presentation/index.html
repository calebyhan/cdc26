Not My DebtHow it works

Four steps. No guessing.

  1. 01

    Extract

    Typed facts, each with its exact source passage. Nothing counts until a person confirms it.

  2. 02

    Match

    Provider, patient, account, and visit date must agree. A matching amount alone is not a match.

  3. 03

    Reconcile

    Integer-cent arithmetic. An EOB is never proof of payment. A payment is counted once.

  4. 04

    Draft

    A letter built only from confirmed facts, citing the records it relies on.

When the evidence is ambiguous, it withholds the balance and says what to confirm.

Next.js + Recharts interface over a Python evidence engine. Local extraction by default; optional AI extraction discloses what it sends.09 / 12
Under the hood, four steps. Extract typed facts with the exact passage they came from, and nothing is used until a person confirms it. Match records on provider, patient, account and visit date. The same dollar amount is never enough. Reconcile with integer-cent arithmetic: an EOB is never treated as proof of payment, and a payment is only counted once. Draft only from confirmed facts. The key design choice is that when evidence is ambiguous, it withholds a balance instead of guessing.

Where it breaks: a bill that labels the provider “Billed by” isn’t read by the local parser. The engine can’t match the account, so it withholds the balance rather than guess. A deliberately broken rule, treating “Pending” as paid, fails the benchmark.

Seeded synthetic documents in the parser’s labeled-text format; expected outcomes follow the product rules. Not an accuracy estimate for real paperwork; no real-user validation yet.11 / 12
We tested it against the ways it could go wrong. We generated 70 case bundles across 14 scenario families: partial payments, duplicate receipts, wrong accounts, masked accounts, pending and reversed payments, payments after the notice, bills that don’t add up. All 70 reach the expected outcome. It never flagged an uncredited payment where one wasn’t expected, including when the payment was already credited, and in all 40 ambiguous cases it withheld a balance. One limitation: the local parser only reads labels it knows, so a bill that says “Billed by” instead of “Provider” fails to match, and the engine abstains rather than guesses. This is a synthetic regression check, not real-world accuracy. Real paperwork is the next test.
